# Gap-Signal Outreach Playbook

**For:** Cybersecurity, GRC and third-party risk (TPRM) advisory: business development and strategic positioning
**Source idea:** A former public affairs consultant describes how they won advisory work by reading government press releases.
**Status:** v1. Adapt the signal sources and sectors to your own pipeline.

---

## 1. The core idea in one paragraph

When government (or a regulator, or a major industry body) announces something, it names a few organisations: who it quoted, who it partnered with, who it consulted. Every organisation in that sector that *wasn't* named is having the same conversation that day: *"Why are they in the room and we're not?"* That moment of exclusion anxiety is the best time to offer help. Their attention is high, they feel urgency, and an internal sponsor (a CEO, CISO or Head of Public Affairs) is suddenly motivated. The consultant's line is the key insight: **the reason your manager is weird with you is the same reason a CEO suddenly writes an op-ed at 9pm.** In both cases it's status, being close to power, and fear of being left out. Learn to read that behaviour and you can predict when an organisation will buy.

### What's taken from the transcript

| Element | What the speaker did | Principle |
|---|---|---|
| Signal | Read government press releases in the policy areas they wanted to work in | Pick a narrow area and watch official announcements |
| Named list | Noted which businesses and trade bodies were quoted | Who's inside the circle is public information |
| Gap list | Worked out who *should* have been mentioned but wasn't | The buyers are the organisations that were left out |
| Timing | Contacted them **the same day** | Urgency fades within 24 to 72 hours |
| Opener | "I'd love to talk about your government engagement. How's it going? What are you finding?" | Ask a question; don't pitch |
| Expectation | Most don't reply, because they're busy having that internal argument | A low reply rate is normal; the replies you do get are high-intent |
| Meta-lesson | Early-career politics taught them to read status anxiety | Organisational psychology *is* strategy |

## 2. How this maps to your work areas

The same pattern works for cyber and GRC advisory. Only the signal sources change.

| Your work area | Signal to watch (the "press release") | Who's named | Who's left out (your target) | Their likely worry | Your opener angle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **GRC / regulatory readiness** | DSIT / NCSC announcements on the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill, NIS updates, Cyber Governance Code of Practice, Ofcom / Ofgem / ICO guidance | Organisations quoted as early adopters or consultees | Peers in the same regulated sector | "Are we behind? Will the regulator see us as a laggard?" | "How are you reading the new obligations for [sector]?" |
| **Third-party risk / TPSA** | A major supplier breach in the news, a regulator fine involving a third party, CCS framework awards | The breached vendor and any named customers | Other customers of that vendor (often visible through case studies and logos) | "Are *we* exposed through that supplier, and could we prove it to the board tomorrow?" | "How confident are you in your supply chain visibility on [vendor]?" |
| **CVE / vulnerability exposure** | CISA KEV additions, NCSC alerts, vendor advisories on widely used products | Organisations publicly confirmed as affected or patched | Others that run the same tech stack | "Is this on our estate? Did our patching cadence slip?" | "How are you prioritising [CVE] across your vendor estate?" |
| **UK public sector / CNI bids** | Contract award notices (Find a Tender, Contracts Finder), NCSC Assured/CAF partner lists, government pilots | The winning supplier and named partners | Unsuccessful bidders and adjacent suppliers | "Why did they win? What's missing from our bids?" | "What did you take from the [award] outcome?" (bid-improvement support) |
| **SOC capability** | Breach disclosures with publicly criticised detection or response times; NCSC annual review case studies | The organisation that was hit, and its named MSSP | Peers on the same MSSP or in the same sector | "Would our SOC have caught that?" | "How would your detection have held up against [incident pattern]?" |
| **AI risk / governance** | AI Security Institute updates, AI Cyber Security Code of Practice, ICO AI guidance, named AI pilot partners | Organisations announced as partners or early signatories | Sector peers deploying AI with no visible governance | "Are we exposed on AI governance without realising it?" | "How's your AI risk assessment going ahead of [guidance]?" |
| **Executive / board advisory** | A peer CEO publishing an op-ed, a CISO appointment, or a board statement after an incident | The organisation and the executive taking a public position | Peers who now look quiet by comparison | "Do we need a public position on this?" | "Are you planning to say anything on [topic]?" (thought-leadership support) |

## 3. The playbook: step by step

### Phase 0: Set up (once, about 1 hour)
1. **Pick 2–3 lanes** from the table above. Narrow lanes beat broad monitoring.
2. **Build a signal list** for each lane: gov.uk news filtered by department (DSIT, Cabinet Office), the NCSC news feed, regulator press pages, CISA KEV, Find a Tender, and trade press.
   *Tip:* gov.uk pages support email alerts and an Atom feed (add `.atom` to most gov.uk search URLs).
3. **Build a peer map** for each target sector: a simple spreadsheet listing the top 20–50 organisations, their trade association, the senior contact roles (CISO, Head of Risk, Head of Public Affairs, COO), and any existing relationship.
4. **Write your 3 openers** in advance (see section 5) so that on the day you only have to personalise them.

### Phase 1: Detect (daily, 10–15 minutes)
5. Scan the signals. Flag any announcement that **names organisations** in one of your lanes.
6. **Log it:** date, source URL, topic, named organisations, and named trade bodies.

### Phase 2: Analyse the gap (15–30 minutes per signal)
7. Compare the named list with your peer map. List who's **missing but should be there**, because of size, market share, sector relevance, or because they've spoken publicly on the topic before.
8. **Score each missing organisation** from 1 to 5 on each of the following, then add the scores up:
   - **Relevance:** how directly the announcement affects them
   - **Status gap:** how bad it looks for them to be left out compared with their peers
   - **Ability to buy:** budget, size, and whether they already use consultants
   - **Access:** whether you know someone there or have a warm route in
   - **Fit:** whether you can genuinely help them on this topic
9. Pick the **top 3–5**. Don't try to contact everyone.

### Phase 3: Reach out (same day, ideally within hours)
10. Send a **short, question-led message** (see section 5). Don't attach a deck or pitch services in the first message.
11. Use the most natural channel: LinkedIn for individuals, email for corporate addresses, or a warm introduction where you have one.
12. **Expect most people not to reply.** The transcript says nine out of ten won't, and that's fine.

### Phase 4: Follow up and convert
13. **Day 3–5:** send one follow-up that offers something useful, such as a one-page "what this means for [sector]" note.
14. **If they reply:** run a discovery conversation (section 6), then propose a **small, fixed-scope first piece of work**, for example a readiness gap assessment, a supplier exposure snapshot, or a board briefing.
15. **If they don't reply:** move them to "nurture" and try again at the next relevant signal. Repeated relevance builds credibility.

### Phase 5: Learn (monthly)
16. Track your results: signals logged, organisations contacted, replies, meetings, proposals, wins. Work out which lanes and openers convert best, and drop the ones that don't.

## 4. Reading the room: why this works (the "weird manager" insight)

The consultant's point is that organisations behave like people do in office politics. Use this to time your outreach and to frame what you say.

| Behaviour you see | What's usually driving it | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| A late-night op-ed or a sudden LinkedIn statement from a CEO | Fear of losing status after a peer got visibility | They've just become a buyer for positioning and engagement advice |
| A sudden RFP or urgent "quick review" request | A board member or regulator asked an awkward question | They need evidence fast, so offer a scoped sprint |
| A trade association speaking for the whole sector | Members rely on it to be heard instead of speaking themselves | Members left out of the room may want their own voice |
| A CISO asking "what are others doing?" | Wanting to know how they compare with peers | Benchmarks and peer comparisons are your best way in |
| Silence after a public incident at a peer | They're privately checking their own exposure | Reach out gently and don't use fear. Offer reassurance work |

**Rule of thumb:** find the person inside the organisation who feels the exclusion most, and help *them* look good to their boss.

## 5. Opener templates

Keep the first message under 80 words. Ask a question and don't pitch.

**A. Regulatory or policy announcement (GRC)**
> Hi [Name], I saw today's [DSIT/NCSC] announcement on [topic]. Organisations like [named peer] were quoted, but I'd expect it to matter just as much for [their organisation]. How are you finding engagement on this so far? I advise [sector] firms on [cyber regulation / CAF readiness] and would be glad to compare notes.

**B. Supplier breach (TPRM)**
> Hi [Name], given today's news about [vendor], I imagine questions are coming up internally about supply chain exposure. How confident do you feel about your visibility into third parties like this? Happy to share how other [sector] teams are approaching it this week.

**C. Contract award (bids)**
> Hi [Name], I saw the [framework/contract] award to [winner] today. I help suppliers strengthen public sector and CNI bids, especially on the security and compliance sections. What did you take from the outcome?

**D. Follow-up with something useful (day 3–5)**
> Following up with a one-page summary of what [announcement] means in practice for [sector]. No ask attached. If it would help to talk it through, I'm around next week.

---

## 6. Discovery call questions (the first 20 minutes)

1. How did today's announcement land internally? Who's been asking about it?
2. Who usually represents you to [government/regulator], and is that working?
3. Where do you feel least able to show your board or regulator you're ready?
4. What would "good" look like in 90 days?
5. Who else needs to be involved for something like this to move forward?

---

## 7. Guardrails (don't skip)

- **Keep outreach legal.** In the UK, unsolicited B2B email to corporate addresses is generally allowed under PECR, but UK GDPR still applies to named individuals. Have a legitimate-interest basis, keep messages relevant to the person's role, give an easy opt-out, and honour it.
- **Don't use fear.** Never exaggerate a threat, imply they've been breached, or claim inside knowledge. Being credible is the whole strategy.
- **Use public information only.** Use what's in public press releases, award notices and disclosures. Never use confidential client information to target someone else, which breaches your NDAs and professional ethics.
- **Check for conflicts.** Check before approaching a direct competitor of a current client.
- **Respect the regulator.** Don't imply a special relationship with government or regulators that you don't have.
- **Don't do any security testing for outreach.** Don't scan or probe a prospect's systems to "find" problems to pitch. That's unauthorised testing, and it's illegal under the Computer Misuse Act. Use passive, public sources only, such as a supplier rating platform you're licensed to use.
